# Article 81 — Suspension of proceedings

**Chapter:** 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

## Summary
Article 81 establishes a mechanism for coordinating parallel judicial proceedings in different Member States involving the same processing subject matter and the same controller or processor. Where a court becomes aware of such parallel proceedings, it must contact the other court to confirm their existence. Courts other than the one first seized may suspend their own proceedings. At first instance, a court may also, on application by one of the parties, decline jurisdiction in favour of the court first seized — provided that court has jurisdiction and its law allows consolidation. The article aims to prevent irreconcilable judgments and ensure efficient use of judicial resources across the EU.

## Key Obligations
- **Court aware of parallel proceedings**: Must contact the court in the other Member State to confirm the existence of proceedings on the same subject matter.
- **Court not first seized**: May suspend proceedings; at first instance, may also decline jurisdiction on application of a party, subject to the conditions above.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Court first seized**: The court in which proceedings were initiated first — acts as the reference point for coordination and potential consolidation.
- **Suspension of proceedings**: A discretionary procedural measure allowing a court to pause its own case pending resolution of related proceedings elsewhere.
- **Declining jurisdiction**: An option at first instance for a court other than the one first seized to relinquish the case, enabling consolidation where that court's law allows it.

## Applies To
National courts of EU Member States, controllers, processors, parties to GDPR litigation.
