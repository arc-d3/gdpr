# Article 78 — Right to an effective judicial remedy against a supervisory authority

**Chapter:** 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

## Summary
Article 78 grants every natural or legal person the right to an effective judicial remedy against a legally binding decision of a supervisory authority that concerns them. Additionally, any data subject may seek a judicial remedy where the competent SA (under Articles 55 or 56) fails to handle their complaint or fails to inform them of the progress or outcome within three months of the complaint being lodged under Article 77. Proceedings must be brought in the Member State where the SA is established. Where a Board opinion or decision in the consistency mechanism preceded the SA's decision, the SA must forward it to the court.

## Key Obligations
- **Supervisory authority**: Must forward any preceding Board opinion or decision to the court when proceedings are brought against a decision that was preceded by such an opinion.
- **Member States**: Must ensure courts are competent to hear challenges to SA decisions and SA failures to act.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Judicial remedy against SA decision**: Right to challenge any legally binding SA decision in court — available to any natural or legal person the decision concerns, not only data subjects.
- **Judicial remedy for SA inaction**: Data subjects may go to court if the SA fails to handle a complaint or does not respond within three months.
- **Competent court**: The court of the Member State where the SA is established — jurisdiction is fixed, not optional.

## Applies To
Natural and legal persons subject to SA decisions, data subjects who lodged complaints, supervisory authorities.
