# Article 80 — Representation of data subjects

**Chapter:** 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

## Summary
Article 80 allows data subjects to mandate a qualifying not-for-profit body to act on their behalf in exercising GDPR enforcement rights. Eligible bodies must be properly constituted under Member State law, have statutory public-interest objectives, and be active in protecting data subjects' rights. A mandated body may lodge complaints (Article 77), seek judicial remedies against SAs (Article 78) or controllers/processors (Article 79), and — where Member State law provides — claim compensation under Article 82. Additionally, Member States may extend this right by allowing qualifying bodies to act independently, without a mandate from a specific data subject, where they consider that GDPR rights have been infringed.

## Key Obligations
- **Not-for-profit body (mandated)**: Must have statutory public-interest objectives and be active in data subjects' rights protection to be eligible for a mandate.
- **Member States**: May (but are not required to) adopt the independent action mechanism under paragraph 2.
- **Controllers/processors**: Must engage with proceedings brought by representative bodies in the same way as proceedings brought directly by data subjects.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Mandate**: An authorisation from a data subject allowing a not-for-profit body to exercise specified Article 77, 78, 79, and/or 82 rights on their behalf.
- **Independent action (Art. 80(2))**: An opt-in mechanism allowing Member States to permit qualifying bodies to bring systemic complaints or proceedings without a specific data subject mandate.
- **Eligibility criteria**: (1) Properly constituted under Member State law; (2) statutory objectives in the public interest; (3) active in the field of data subjects' rights protection.

## Applies To
Data subjects, not-for-profit bodies and associations, supervisory authorities, controllers, processors, Member States.
