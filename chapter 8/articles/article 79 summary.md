# Article 79 — Right to an effective judicial remedy against a controller or processor

**Chapter:** 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

## Summary
Article 79 gives every data subject the right to bring court proceedings directly against a controller or processor where they believe their GDPR rights have been infringed by non-compliant processing. This right is available without prejudice to any administrative remedy, including the right to lodge a complaint with an SA under Article 77. Data subjects have a choice of forum: proceedings may be brought in the Member State where the controller or processor has an establishment, or alternatively in the Member State of the data subject's habitual residence — unless the controller/processor is a public authority acting in its public powers, in which case the alternative forum is not available.

## Key Obligations
- **Controller/processor**: Must be prepared to defend proceedings in the Member State of their establishment or, where invoked, the Member State of the data subject's habitual residence.
- **Member States**: Must ensure courts have jurisdiction to hear such proceedings and provide effective remedies.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Right to effective judicial remedy**: A direct right of action against the controller or processor in court — independent of and complementary to the SA complaint route.
- **Choice of forum**: Data subjects may sue where the controller/processor is established or where they themselves habitually reside (subject to the public authority exception).
- **Public authority exception**: Where the controller/processor is a public authority acting in the exercise of its public powers, the data subject cannot rely on the habitual residence forum.

## Applies To
Data subjects, controllers, processors.
