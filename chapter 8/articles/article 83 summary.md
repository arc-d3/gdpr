### Supervisory authority — imposing fines
- [ ] Ensure any fine imposed is effective, proportionate, and dissuasive in each individual case
- [ ] Consider imposing fines in addition to, or instead of, other corrective measures under Article 58(2)(a)–(h) and (j)
- [ ] Evaluate all relevant factors before deciding whether to impose a fine and its amount:
  - [ ] Nature, gravity and duration of the infringement; number of data subjects affected and level of damage
  - [ ] Intentional or negligent character of the infringement
  - [ ] Actions taken by the controller/processor to mitigate damage
  - [ ] Degree of responsibility, including technical and organisational measures under Articles 25 and 32
  - [ ] Prior infringements by the same controller or processor
  - [ ] Degree of cooperation with the SA to remedy the infringement and mitigate adverse effects
  - [ ] Categories of personal data affected
  - [ ] How the infringement became known to the SA and whether it was notified by the controller/processor
  - [ ] Prior measures ordered under Article 58(2) against the same controller/processor on the same subject matter
  - [ ] Adherence to approved codes of conduct (Article 40) or certification mechanisms (Article 42)
  - [ ] Any other aggravating or mitigating factors, including financial benefits gained or losses avoided
- [ ] For multiple linked infringements, cap the total fine at the amount for the gravest infringement
- [ ] Apply Tier 1 (up to €10 million / 2% global turnover) for infringements of Articles 8, 11, 25–39, 42, 43, and obligations of certification or monitoring bodies
- [ ] Apply Tier 2 (up to €20 million / 4% global turnover) for infringements of basic processing principles (Articles 5–7, 9), data subject rights (Articles 12–22), international transfer rules (Articles 44–49), Chapter IX obligations, or non-compliance with SA orders under Article 58(2)
- [ ] Apply the public authority fine rules as determined by Member State law (Article 83(7))
- [ ] Ensure all fine decisions are subject to appropriate procedural safeguards, effective judicial remedy, and due process
- [ ] Where national law does not provide for administrative fines, apply the Article 83(9) mechanism via competent national courts

# Article 83 — General conditions for imposing administrative fines

**Chapter:** 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

## Summary
Article 83 sets out the framework for supervisory authorities to impose administrative fines. All fines must be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. The article provides eleven factors for determining whether to impose a fine and its amount, covering the seriousness of the infringement, intent, mitigating actions, prior conduct, and cooperation. Where a controller or processor commits multiple linked infringements, the total fine is capped at the amount for the gravest. Two penalty tiers apply: Tier 1 (up to €10 million / 2% global annual turnover) for procedural and controller/processor obligation violations, and Tier 2 (up to €20 million / 4% global annual turnover) for violations of core principles, data subject rights, international transfer rules, and non-compliance with SA orders. Member States retain discretion on fines for public authorities. Where national legal systems do not permit administrative fines, Member States must apply an equivalent mechanism via national courts.

## Key Obligations
- **Supervisory authorities**: Must ensure fines are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive; must apply the eleven factors when deciding on fines; must observe procedural safeguards.
- **Member States**: Must set rules on fines for public authorities (Article 83(7)); where no administrative fine system exists, must implement an equivalent court-based mechanism and notify the Commission.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Tier 1 fines (Art. 83(4))**: Up to €10,000,000 or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher) — for violations of Articles 8, 11, 25–39, 42, 43 and obligations of certification/monitoring bodies.
- **Tier 2 fines (Art. 83(5)–(6))**: Up to €20,000,000 or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover (whichever is higher) — for violations of core processing principles (Articles 5–7, 9), data subject rights (Articles 12–22), international transfer rules (Articles 44–49), Chapter IX obligations, and non-compliance with Article 58(2) SA orders.
- **Effective, proportionate, dissuasive**: The three mandatory characteristics every fine must satisfy regardless of tier.

## Applies To
Supervisory authorities, controllers, processors, certification bodies, monitoring bodies, Member States.
