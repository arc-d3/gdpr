# Chapter 8 — Remedies, Liability and Penalties

**Articles:** Articles 77–84

## Purpose
Chapter 8 establishes the enforcement mechanisms available to data subjects and third parties when GDPR rights are violated. It sets out three parallel tracks of redress: administrative complaints to supervisory authorities, judicial remedies against supervisory authorities or controllers/processors, and financial compensation. It also defines the conditions and tiers for administrative fines and requires Member States to enact additional national penalties for infringements not covered by fines.

## Articles at a Glance
- **Article 77** — *Right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority*: Every data subject may lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority if they believe processing of their personal data infringes the GDPR.
- **Article 78** — *Right to an effective judicial remedy against a supervisory authority*: Natural or legal persons may seek a judicial remedy against a legally binding SA decision, or where an SA fails to act on a complaint within three months.
- **Article 79** — *Right to an effective judicial remedy against a controller or processor*: Data subjects may bring court proceedings directly against a controller or processor where they believe their GDPR rights have been infringed.
- **Article 80** — *Representation of data subjects*: Data subjects may mandate a qualifying not-for-profit body to lodge complaints and exercise rights on their behalf; Member States may allow such bodies to act independently.
- **Article 81** — *Suspension of proceedings*: Courts in different Member States dealing with the same processing subject matter may coordinate proceedings; courts other than the one first seized may suspend or decline jurisdiction.
- **Article 82** — *Right to compensation and liability*: Any person who suffers material or non-material damage due to a GDPR infringement has the right to compensation from the controller or processor; liability rules and exemptions are defined.
- **Article 83** — *General conditions for imposing administrative fines*: Lays down criteria for imposing fines, two penalty tiers (up to €10 million / 2% and up to €20 million / 4% of global turnover), and ensures fines are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive.
- **Article 84** — *Penalties*: Requires Member States to establish additional national penalties for infringements not covered by Article 83 administrative fines and to notify the Commission.

## Key Themes
- **Multiple redress tracks**: Data subjects can pursue complaints (SA), court action (against SA or controller/processor), and compensation simultaneously.
- **Joint and several liability**: Where multiple controllers or processors are responsible, each is liable for the full damage to ensure effective compensation.
- **Proportionate fines**: Fines must account for the nature, gravity, duration, and intent of the infringement, as well as mitigating or aggravating factors.
- **Representation**: Third-party bodies can act on behalf of data subjects, strengthening collective enforcement.
- **Coordination across borders**: Article 81 prevents conflicting judgments through cross-border judicial cooperation.

## Practical Implications
- Organisations must be prepared to face complaints filed with any SA in the EU where a data subject resides, works, or where the alleged infringement occurred.
- Serious GDPR violations (e.g., unlawful basis for processing, breaches of data subject rights, illegal international transfers) can attract fines of up to €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover.
- Controllers and processors face joint and several liability for damages; internal indemnity arrangements between co-responsible parties are allowed.
- Not-for-profit data protection advocacy bodies can independently bring systemic cases in Member States that have adopted Article 80(2).
- Organisations should establish clear internal procedures for responding to SA complaints, court proceedings, and compensation claims.
