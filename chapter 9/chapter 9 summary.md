# Chapter 9 — Provisions Relating to Specific Processing Situations

## Overview
Chapter 9 (Articles 85–91) grants Member States the flexibility to adapt GDPR rules to specific sectors and contexts where other fundamental rights or practical necessities require a different balance. It covers freedom of expression, access to public documents, national identification numbers, employment, research/archiving/statistics, professional secrecy, and the status of churches. Rather than imposing uniform EU-wide rules, most articles delegate law-making authority to Member States within defined boundaries.

## Articles in this Chapter

| Article | Title |
|---------|-------|
| 85 | Processing and freedom of expression and information |
| 86 | Processing and public access to official documents |
| 87 | Processing of the national identification number |
| 88 | Processing in the context of employment |
| 89 | Safeguards and derogations for archiving, research, and statistics |
| 90 | Obligations of secrecy |
| 91 | Existing data protection rules of churches and religious associations |

## Key Themes
- **Member State flexibility**: Almost every article in Chapter 9 delegates rule-making to Member States, allowing national law to fine-tune GDPR obligations to local constitutional, social, or institutional contexts.
- **Balancing competing rights**: The chapter consistently frames data protection as one right among several (freedom of expression, public transparency, employment rights, religious autonomy) that must be reconciled rather than treated as absolute.
- **Safeguards remain mandatory**: Even where derogations are permitted, appropriate safeguards — especially data minimisation, pseudonymisation, and independent oversight — are always required.
- **Commission notification**: Several articles (85, 88, 90) require Member States to notify the Commission of national laws adopted under them by 25 May 2018 and of subsequent amendments.
