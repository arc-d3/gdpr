# Chapter 1 – General Provisions (Art. 1–4)

## Overview

Chapter 1 lays the constitutional foundation of the GDPR. It defines **why** the Regulation exists, **what** it covers, **where** it applies, and **what key terms** mean throughout the entire Regulation. Before any compliance obligation or right can be understood, these four articles must be mastered.

## Chapter Summary

**Article 1** establishes the GDPR's dual purpose: protecting individuals' fundamental right to data protection, and ensuring the free flow of personal data within the EU internal market. These two goals coexist — data protection rights cannot be used to block legitimate data flows.

**Article 2** defines the material scope. The GDPR applies to automated processing and to structured manual filing systems. Key exclusions include purely personal/household activities, national security and law enforcement operations, and activities outside EU law.

**Article 3** gives the GDPR its broad extraterritorial reach. It applies not only to EU-based organisations, but also to any non-EU organisation that offers goods/services to EU residents or monitors their behaviour — regardless of where the organisation is based.

**Article 4** provides 26 legally precise definitions that underpin the whole Regulation. The most critical are: *personal data* (broad — any info about an identifiable person), *processing* (broad — almost any operation on data), *controller* (decides the why and how), *processor* (acts on the controller's behalf), and *consent* (must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous).

---

## Key Takeaways

- The GDPR protects people, not data for its own sake — the person (data subject) is central.
- Scope is intentionally wide: automated + manual structured data, EU + non-EU organisations.
- The definitions in Art. 4 are not optional background knowledge — they directly determine whether the GDPR applies to a given situation and who bears responsibility.
