# Article 4 – Definitions

**Chapter:** 1 – General Provisions  
**Link:** https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/

---

## Summary

Article 4 provides 26 legally binding definitions that are used throughout the entire GDPR. These are not merely glossary entries — they directly determine whether the Regulation applies, who is responsible, and what rules must be followed. The most important definitions are:

- **Personal data (1):** Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Intentionally broad — names, IDs, location data, online identifiers, and even indirect identifiers all count.
- **Processing (2):** Any operation on personal data — collection, storage, use, disclosure, erasure, etc. Almost any interaction with personal data is "processing."
- **Controller (7):** The entity that decides *why* and *how* data is processed. Bears primary GDPR responsibility.
- **Processor (8):** An entity that processes data *on behalf of* the controller. Has direct obligations under the GDPR.
- **Consent (11):** Must be **freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous**. Pre-ticked boxes, silence, and bundled consent do not qualify.
- **Personal data breach (12):** Any security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, or unauthorised access to personal data.
- **Special category data (13–15):** Genetic data, biometric data, and health data — these trigger heightened protections under Art. 9.
- **Profiling (4):** Automated processing to evaluate personal aspects of a person (e.g. behaviour, health, preferences).
- **Pseudonymisation (5):** Replacing identifiers so data can no longer be attributed to a specific person without additional information held separately.

## Legal Text

> **(1) 'personal data'** — any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person; an identifiable person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, identification number, location data, or online identifier.
>
> **(2) 'processing'** — any operation performed on personal data, such as collection, recording, storage, adaptation, retrieval, use, disclosure, combination, restriction, erasure or destruction.
>
> **(4) 'profiling'** — automated processing of personal data to evaluate personal aspects, in particular to analyse or predict behaviour, health, preferences, location, etc.
>
> **(5) 'pseudonymisation'** — processing personal data so it can no longer be attributed to a specific person without additional separately-held information.
>
> **(7) 'controller'** — the entity that determines the purposes and means of processing personal data.
>
> **(8) 'processor'** — an entity that processes personal data on behalf of the controller.
>
> **(11) 'consent'** — any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of wishes, by a statement or clear affirmative action.
>
> **(12) 'personal data breach'** — a breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data.
>
> *(Full text of all 26 definitions at https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)*

## Todo List

- [ ] Learn the definition of **personal data** — test edge cases: is an IP address personal data? (Yes, per CJEU). Is anonymised data? (No, if truly irreversible).
- [ ] Confirm which entities in your organisation are **controllers** vs **processors** — document this in contracts and the RoPA.
- [ ] Review all **consent** mechanisms used — ensure they meet all four criteria (freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous). Remove pre-ticked boxes.
- [ ] Audit your **data breach response process** — the definition in Art. 4(12) triggers the 72-hour notification duty in Art. 33.
- [ ] Identify any use of **profiling** in your systems — this may trigger Art. 22 (automated decision-making) obligations.
- [ ] Map out which data you hold qualifies as **genetic, biometric, or health data** — these are special categories under Art. 9.
- [ ] Understand **pseudonymisation** vs **anonymisation** — pseudonymised data is still personal data; truly anonymised data is not.
- [ ] Identify your **main establishment** in the EU — this determines your lead supervisory authority under the one-stop-shop (Art. 56).
- [ ] If non-EU: designate an **EU Representative** as defined in Art. 4(17), required by Art. 27.
- [ ] If using **binding corporate rules** (Art. 4(20)) for international transfers, ensure they are approved by the competent DPA.
