# Article 3 – Territorial Scope

**Chapter:** 1 – General Provisions  
**Link:** https://gdpr-info.eu/art-3-gdpr/

---

## Summary

Article 3 defines *where* the GDPR applies — and it reaches far beyond the EU's borders. There are three triggers:

1. **Establishment principle (Art. 3(1)):** Any organisation with an establishment in the EU is subject to GDPR for processing done *in the context of* that establishment's activities — even if the actual processing happens outside the EU.

2. **Targeting/monitoring principle (Art. 3(2)):** Non-EU organisations are subject to GDPR if they:
   - **Offer goods or services** to people in the EU (payment is not required — free services count), OR
   - **Monitor the behaviour** of people in the EU (e.g. tracking via cookies, profiling).

3. **International law principle (Art. 3(3)):** Applies where a Member State's law governs by virtue of international law (e.g. an embassy or consulate).

This article is why GDPR is considered a global standard — any organisation worldwide that targets EU residents must comply.

---

## Legal Text

> **1.** This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data in the context of the activities of an establishment of a controller or a processor in the Union, regardless of whether the processing takes place in the Union or not.
>
> **2.** This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data of data subjects who are in the Union by a controller or processor not established in the Union, where the processing activities are related to:
> - **(a)** the offering of goods or services, irrespective of whether a payment of the data subject is required, to such data subjects in the Union; or
> - **(b)** the monitoring of their behaviour as far as their behaviour takes place within the Union.
>
> **3.** This Regulation applies to the processing of personal data by a controller not established in the Union, but in a place where Member State law applies by virtue of public international law.

- [ ] Document your territorial scope determination in your Records of Processing Activities (RoPA).
