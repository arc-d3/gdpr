# Chapter 5 GDPR – Transfers of Personal Data to Third Countries or International Organisations (Art. 44–50)

## Overview

Chapter 5 governs all **cross-border transfers** of personal data to recipients outside the EU/EEA. The core principle is that the level of protection guaranteed by the GDPR must travel with the data — transfers are only lawful if one of the mechanisms defined in this chapter applies. The chapter is structured as a hierarchy of transfer tools, moving from the most straightforward (adequacy decision) to last-resort derogations.

| Article | Topic |
|---------|-------|
| Art. 44 | General principle — all Chapter 5 conditions must be met |
| Art. 45 | Transfers based on a Commission adequacy decision |
| Art. 46 | Transfers subject to appropriate safeguards (SCCs, BCRs, codes of conduct, certifications) |
| Art. 47 | Binding corporate rules (BCRs) |
| Art. 48 | Transfers not authorised by Union law (foreign court orders) |
| Art. 49 | Derogations for specific situations (consent, contract, public interest, etc.) |
| Art. 50 | International cooperation between supervisory authorities |

---

## Article Summaries

### Art. 44 – General Principle for Transfers
Every transfer (including onward transfers) to a third country or international organisation must comply with this Chapter. No provision of this Chapter may be circumvented, and the level of GDPR protection must not be undermined.

### Art. 45 – Transfers on the Basis of an Adequacy Decision
Where the Commission has determined that a third country, territory, sector, or international organisation provides an **adequate level of protection**, transfers may flow freely without additional authorisation. Adequacy is assessed against rule of law, human rights, data protection legislation, independent oversight, and international commitments. The Commission must review adequacy decisions at least every four years and may repeal or amend them if the standard is no longer met.

### Art. 46 – Transfers Subject to Appropriate Safeguards
In the absence of an adequacy decision, transfers are permitted if the controller or processor provides **appropriate safeguards** and data subjects have enforceable rights and effective legal remedies. Safeguards that require no supervisory authority authorisation include: legally binding instruments between public bodies, BCRs (Art. 47), standard contractual clauses (SCCs) adopted by the Commission or a supervisory authority, approved codes of conduct, and approved certifications. Safeguards requiring supervisory authority authorisation include bespoke contractual clauses and provisions in administrative arrangements.

### Art. 47 – Binding Corporate Rules
BCRs allow intra-group transfers within a multinational corporate group. The lead supervisory authority approves BCRs via the consistency mechanism (Art. 63). BCRs must be legally binding, cover all group members and employees, expressly confer enforceable rights on data subjects, and contain detailed provisions on structure, liability, transparency, data protection principles, DPO tasks, complaint procedures, audit mechanisms, change-notification, and training.

### Art. 48 – Transfers or Disclosures Not Authorised by Union Law
Foreign court judgments or administrative decisions requiring a controller/processor to transfer or disclose personal data are **only enforceable** if they are based on an international agreement (e.g., mutual legal assistance treaty) in force between the requesting country and the EU or a Member State. This article acts as a shield against extraterritorial demands inconsistent with EU law.

### Art. 49 – Derogations for Specific Situations
When neither an adequacy decision nor appropriate safeguards exist, transfers are only permitted under specific derogations: explicit informed consent of the data subject; necessity for contract performance or pre-contractual steps; necessity for a contract in the data subject's interest; important public interest reasons; establishment/exercise/defence of legal claims; protection of vital interests when the data subject cannot consent; or transfer from a public register. A residual derogation exists for non-repetitive transfers involving compelling legitimate interests, subject to documentation and notification to the supervisory authority.

### Art. 50 – International Cooperation for the Protection of Personal Data
The Commission and supervisory authorities must take appropriate steps to develop international cooperation mechanisms, provide mutual assistance, engage stakeholders, and promote the exchange of data protection legislation and practice with third countries and international organisations.
