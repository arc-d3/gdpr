# Art. 49 GDPR – Derogations for Specific Situations

## Summary

Article 49 provides **last-resort derogations** that permit transfers to third countries when neither an adequacy decision (Art. 45) nor appropriate safeguards (Art. 46) are available. Because these derogations are exceptions to the rule, they must be interpreted restrictively and used only occasionally — not as a routine transfer mechanism.

| Derogation | Condition |
|------------|-----------|
| **(a) Explicit consent** | Data subject has explicitly consented after being informed of the specific risks due to the absence of an adequacy decision and safeguards. |
| **(b) Contract performance** | Transfer necessary for performing a contract between the data subject and the controller, or for implementing pre-contractual measures at the data subject's request. |
| **(c) Third-party contract** | Transfer necessary for concluding or performing a contract in the data subject's interest between the controller and a third party. |
| **(d) Public interest** | Transfer necessary for important reasons of public interest recognised in Union or Member State law. |
| **(e) Legal claims** | Transfer necessary for the establishment, exercise, or defence of legal claims. |
| **(f) Vital interests** | Transfer necessary to protect vital interests of the data subject or others, where the data subject is physically or legally incapable of giving consent. |
| **(g) Public register** | Transfer from a register intended by Union or Member State law to be publicly consulted, to the extent the conditions for consultation are met. |
| **Residual derogation** | Non-repetitive transfer for compelling legitimate interests of the controller not overridden by data subject interests/rights/freedoms; controller must assess circumstances, provide suitable safeguards, document the assessment, notify the supervisory authority, and inform the data subject. |

**Additional rules:**
- Register transfers (derogation (g)) must not involve the entirety of the data or entire categories; if the register is for persons with legitimate interest, the transfer is made only at their request.
- Derogations (a), (b), (c) and the residual derogation do **not** apply to public authorities acting in their public powers.
- Public interest under (d) must be recognised in Union or Member State law; Member States must notify the Commission of any limiting provisions.
- Documentation of residual derogation assessments and safeguards must be recorded in the Art. 30 records.

## Todo List

- [ ] Confirm that Art. 49 derogations are used only as a **last resort** — verify that no adequacy decision or Art. 46 safeguard is available before invoking any derogation.
- [ ] For **consent-based transfers** (Art. 49(1)(a)): update consent mechanisms to specifically inform data subjects of the third-country transfer risks, and ensure consent is explicit, freely given, and separately recorded.
- [ ] For **contract-performance transfers** (Art. 49(1)(b)/(c)): document the necessity link between the transfer and the contract; confirm the transfer is not merely convenient but genuinely required.
- [ ] For **public interest transfers** (Art. 49(1)(d)): obtain a legal opinion confirming the public interest basis is recognised in Union or Member State law; document the legal reference.
- [ ] For **legal claims transfers** (Art. 49(1)(e)): limit the transfer to the data strictly necessary for the claim; document the legal proceedings or anticipated claims justifying the transfer.
- [ ] For **vital interests transfers** (Art. 49(1)(f)): ensure this is used only when the data subject is demonstrably incapable of giving consent; document the circumstances.
- [ ] For **public register transfers** (Art. 49(1)(g)): verify the register qualifies under Union or Member State law; do not transfer entire datasets or categories.
- [ ] For the **residual derogation**: document the compelling legitimate interests assessment, the suitable safeguards implemented, notify the competent supervisory authority before the transfer, and inform the data subject.
- [ ] Record all Art. 49 derogation uses (including the residual derogation assessments) in the Records of Processing Activities (Art. 30).
- [ ] Periodically audit transfers relying on Art. 49 derogations to confirm they have not become repetitive or structural — if so, implement a formal Art. 46 mechanism instead.
- [ ] Public authorities must ensure derogations (a), (b), (c), and the residual clause are not used for transfers in the exercise of public powers.
