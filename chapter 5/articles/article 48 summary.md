# Art. 48 GDPR – Transfers or Disclosures Not Authorised by Union Law

## Summary

Article 48 is a **defensive provision** protecting personal data held in the EU from compulsory disclosure demanded by foreign courts, tribunals, or administrative authorities. Such demands — for example, orders under US discovery rules or foreign anti-corruption laws — may only be recognised and enforced if they are grounded in a **valid international agreement** (e.g., a mutual legal assistance treaty, MLAT) in force between the requesting country and the EU or a Member State.

| Key Point | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Scope** | Any foreign court judgment or administrative decision requiring a controller or processor to transfer or disclose personal data. |
| **Condition for enforceability** | Must be based on an international agreement (e.g., MLAT) in force between the requesting third country and the EU or a Member State. |
| **Without prejudice** | Does not affect other Chapter 5 transfer grounds (Art. 46–49); a voluntary transfer under another basis remains possible. |
| **Purpose** | Prevents extraterritorial demands from bypassing GDPR transfer requirements. |

- [ ] Verify whether a valid international agreement (MLAT or equivalent) exists between the requesting country and the EU or relevant Member State before responding to any foreign demand.

- [ ] Train legal, compliance, and IT security teams to recognise and properly handle foreign compulsory disclosure requests.
- [ ] Include Art. 48 handling obligations in data processor agreements — processors must notify the controller immediately upon receiving a foreign disclosure demand.
- [ ] Consult the competent supervisory authority where there is doubt about whether a foreign request can legally be complied with under EU law.
