# Article 57 — Tasks

**Chapter:** 6 — Independent Supervisory Authorities

## Summary
Article 57 sets out the comprehensive catalogue of tasks that every supervisory authority must carry out within its territory. These tasks span the full lifecycle of data protection governance: from monitoring and enforcement, to public awareness and education, to handling individual complaints, to cooperative work with other SAs and EU institutions, to the adoption of standard clauses and approval of codes of conduct and certification schemes. Services to data subjects and data protection officers must generally be provided free of charge. SAs may charge fees or refuse to act only on requests that are manifestly unfounded or excessive, but bear the burden of demonstrating this.

## Key Obligations
- Monitor and enforce GDPR on the SA's territory; cooperate with and assist other SAs.
- Promote awareness among the public (with special focus on children), controllers, and processors.
- Advise national legislative and executive bodies on data protection matters.
- Handle complaints and conduct investigations; keep complainants informed of progress.
- Adopt standard contractual clauses; maintain DPIA lists; advise on prior consultation cases.
- Promote, approve, and periodically review codes of conduct and certification schemes.
- Accredit monitoring and certification bodies; approve binding corporate rules.
- Contribute to the EDPB; maintain internal records of infringements and remedial measures.
- Perform tasks free of charge; fees permitted only for manifestly unfounded or excessive requests (burden of proof on the SA).

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Manifestly unfounded or excessive request**: A threshold (particularly based on repetition) that allows the SA to charge a fee or refuse action — but the SA must prove the threshold is met.

## Applies To
Supervisory authorities, controllers, processors, data subjects, certification bodies, code-monitoring bodies.
