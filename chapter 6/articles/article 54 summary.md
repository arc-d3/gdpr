# Article 54 — Rules on the Establishment of the Supervisory Authority

**Chapter:** 6 — Independent Supervisory Authorities

## Summary
Article 54 requires each Member State to enshrine in national law a comprehensive statutory framework for every supervisory authority. This includes the authority's formal establishment, the qualifications required of its members, appointment procedures, term length (at least four years), reappointment rules, and conduct obligations that apply both during and after the term of office. It also mandates a duty of professional secrecy: members and staff must keep confidential information obtained in the course of their work secret — a duty that is particularly important for protecting individuals who report GDPR violations to the SA.

## Key Obligations
- Member States must legislate: (a) the formal establishment of each SA; (b) member qualifications and eligibility; (c) appointment rules and procedures; (d) a term of office of no less than four years (shorter initial terms for staggered appointment are permitted); (e) reappointment eligibility and limits; (f) obligations, incompatibility rules, and post-office restrictions for members and staff; (g) rules on cessation of employment.
- SA members and staff are subject to a **duty of professional secrecy** during and after their term, covering all confidential information obtained in the course of their duties.
- The secrecy obligation explicitly covers information received from persons who report GDPR infringements to the SA.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Professional secrecy**: A binding legal obligation preventing SA members and staff from disclosing confidential information acquired in the exercise of their functions, including after they leave office.

## Applies To
Member States, supervisory authorities, SA members and staff.
