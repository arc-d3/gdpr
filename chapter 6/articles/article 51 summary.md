# Article 51 — Supervisory Authority

**Chapter:** 6 — Independent Supervisory Authorities

## Summary
Article 51 requires every EU Member State to establish one or more independent public authorities whose mission is to monitor and enforce the application of the GDPR. These authorities — collectively called "supervisory authorities" — exist to protect the fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons with respect to data processing and to facilitate the free flow of personal data within the Union. Where a Member State sets up more than one supervisory authority, it must designate which one represents the others in the European Data Protection Board and must ensure compliance with the GDPR consistency mechanism. Each Member State must also notify the Commission of its implementing national provisions.

## Key Obligations
- Each Member State must establish one or more independent public authorities responsible for monitoring the GDPR.
- Supervisory authorities must cooperate with each other and with the Commission to ensure consistent application across the EU.
- Where multiple SAs exist in one Member State, a representative SA for the Board must be designated and a compliance mechanism for the consistency rules must be set out.
- Member States must notify the Commission of relevant national legal provisions by 25 May 2018 and promptly for any subsequent amendments.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Supervisory authority**: An independent public authority responsible for monitoring the application of the GDPR so as to protect fundamental rights and freedoms of natural persons in relation to processing.

## Applies To
Controllers, processors, Member States, supervisory authorities.
