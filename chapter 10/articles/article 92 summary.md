# Article 92 — Exercise of the Delegation

**Chapter:** 10 — Delegated Acts and Implementing Acts

## Summary
Article 92 sets out the conditions under which the European Commission may exercise the power to adopt delegated acts under the GDPR. The delegation covers the two specific powers granted by Article 12(8) (standardised icons for transparency) and Article 43(8) (certification criteria). The delegation is granted for an indefinite period from 24 May 2016, but can be revoked at any time by the European Parliament or the Council. Before any delegated act enters into force, neither the Parliament nor the Council must raise an objection within three months of being notified (extendable by a further three months).

## Key Points
- **Scope of delegation**: Applies only to Article 12(8) (standardised information icons) and Article 43(8) (certification body accreditation criteria).
- **Duration**: Indefinite, starting 24 May 2016.
- **Revocation**: Either the European Parliament or the Council may revoke the delegation at any time; revocation ends the delegation but does not invalidate delegated acts already in force.
- **Simultaneous notification**: The Commission must notify both the Parliament and the Council immediately upon adopting a delegated act.
- **Objection window**: A delegated act only enters into force if neither institution objects within three months of notification; both institutions may waive their right to object early, or extend the period by a further three months.
