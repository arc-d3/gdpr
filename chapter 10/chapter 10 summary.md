# Chapter 10 — Delegated Acts and Implementing Acts

## Overview
Chapter 10 (Articles 92–93) governs how the European Commission may exercise secondary law-making powers under the GDPR. It distinguishes between two types of acts: **delegated acts** (quasi-legislative measures filling in non-essential elements of the regulation) and **implementing acts** (measures ensuring uniform application). The chapter keeps these powers tightly scoped — only two specific GDPR provisions trigger the delegation — and subjects them to rigorous parliamentary and inter-institutional oversight.

## Articles in this Chapter

| Article | Title |
|---------|-------|
| 92 | Exercise of the delegation |
| 93 | Committee procedure |

## Key Themes
- **Limited delegation**: The Commission's power to adopt delegated acts is confined to Article 12(8) (standardised transparency icons) and Article 43(8) (certification body accreditation criteria) — no broader law-making authority is granted.
- **Democratic oversight**: The European Parliament and the Council may revoke the delegation or object to individual delegated acts within a three-month window, preserving democratic control over the Commission.
- **Comitology discipline**: Implementing acts must go through the committee procedure under Regulation (EU) No 182/2011, ensuring Member State experts participate in and can block measures they oppose.
- **Urgency safety valve**: The special/urgency procedure under Article 8 of Reg. 182/2011 is available for immediately applicable implementing acts in exceptional circumstances.
