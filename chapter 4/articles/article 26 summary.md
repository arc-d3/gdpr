# Art. 26 GDPR – Joint Controllers

## Summary

Article 26 applies where two or more controllers **jointly determine the purposes and means** of processing, making them joint controllers. They must arrange their respective compliance responsibilities transparently and communicate the arrangement's essence to data subjects.

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Joint determination test** | Two or more entities that together decide the purposes and means of processing are joint controllers — regardless of how they describe the relationship. |
| **Arrangement** | Must transparently allocate responsibilities for GDPR compliance, in particular: data subject rights fulfilment, and providing Art. 13/14 privacy notices. |
| **Form** | The arrangement may be a contract or any other binding agreement; must duly reflect the real roles and relationships of the parties. |
| **Contact point** | The arrangement may designate a single contact point for data subjects. |
| **Essence to data subjects** | The essence of the arrangement must be made available to data subjects. |
| **Data subject rights** | Data subjects may exercise their rights against each of the joint controllers irrespective of the arrangement. |

## Todo List

- [ ] Map all processing activities to identify where another organisation **jointly determines purposes and means** with your organisation (e.g. co-marketing, shared platforms, analytics partnerships).
- [ ] For each identified joint controller relationship, negotiate and sign a **joint controller arrangement** (Art. 26(1)) covering the allocation of GDPR compliance responsibilities.
- [ ] Ensure the arrangement clearly assigns responsibility for: **data subject rights requests**, **privacy notices** (Art. 13/14), **breach notification**, and **DPA obligations**.
- [ ] Designate a **single contact point** for data subjects in the joint controller arrangement where practical.
- [ ] Make the **essence of each arrangement** available to data subjects (e.g. in the privacy notice or a published summary).
- [ ] Confirm that data subjects can **exercise rights against your organisation** directly, regardless of internal allocation under the arrangement.
- [ ] Review arrangements periodically to ensure they remain accurate as the processing relationship evolves.
- [ ] Train relevant teams (legal, product, marketing) to recognise joint controller scenarios before entering new partnerships.
