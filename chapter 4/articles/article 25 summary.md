# Art. 25 GDPR – Data Protection by Design and by Default

## Summary

Article 25 requires controllers to integrate data protection into processing systems and practices **at the design stage** (privacy by design) and to ensure that, **by default**, only the minimum personal data necessary for each specific purpose are processed (privacy by default).

| Concept | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Privacy by design** | At the time of determining the means of processing and at the time of processing itself, implement TOMs (e.g. pseudonymisation) to embed data-protection principles such as data minimisation. |
| **Privacy by default** | Only personal data necessary for each specific purpose are processed by default — covering the amount collected, extent of processing, storage period, and accessibility. |
| **Accessibility default** | Personal data must not be made accessible to an indefinite number of persons without the individual's active intervention. |
| **State of the art** | The cost of implementation and the state of the art must be considered when choosing measures. |
| **Certification** | An approved certification mechanism under Art. 42 may be used to demonstrate compliance with this article. |

## Todo List

- [ ] Establish a **privacy by design checklist** that is embedded into the system development lifecycle (SDLC) and procurement processes for all new processing activities.
- [ ] Review existing systems to identify where **data minimisation** can be improved (reduce data collected, extent of use, retention periods).
- [ ] Configure all systems so that **privacy-protective settings are the default** (e.g. opt-in rather than opt-out for non-essential processing; most restrictive visibility by default).
- [ ] Document **pseudonymisation and encryption** choices for each major processing activity and justify why they are appropriate to the risk.
- [ ] Include a **privacy by design sign-off** step in new project/product approval gates, with DPO involvement where required.
- [ ] Assess whether existing user-facing settings expose data to unnecessary audiences and remediate where needed.
- [ ] Evaluate whether **certification under Art. 42** is appropriate to demonstrate Art. 25 compliance to customers or partners.
- [ ] Train development, engineering, and product teams on Art. 25 requirements and the organisation's privacy-by-design standards.
