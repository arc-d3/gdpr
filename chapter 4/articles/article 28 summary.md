# Art. 28 GDPR – Processor

## Summary

Article 28 governs the **controller–processor relationship**. Controllers may only engage processors that provide sufficient guarantees of GDPR compliance, and the relationship must be governed by a binding **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)**. Sub-processing requires prior written authorisation from the controller. A processor that acts outside its instructions and determines its own purposes is reclassified as a controller.

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Selection** | Controllers must only use processors offering sufficient guarantees (TOMs and GDPR compliance). |
| **Sub-processor authorisation** | Specific or general written authorisation required; general authorisation requires notice of changes and right to object. |
| **DPA mandatory content** | Subject-matter, duration, nature/purpose, type of data, categories of data subjects, controller's obligations and rights. |
| **Processor obligations in DPA** | (a) process on documented instructions only; (b) ensure confidentiality; (c) implement Art. 32 security; (d) comply with sub-processor rules; (e) assist with data subject rights; (f) assist with Art. 32–36; (g) delete or return data on termination; (h) provide audit access and information. |
| **Sub-processor chain** | Same DPA obligations must flow down to sub-processors; initial processor remains liable for sub-processor failures. |
| **Standard contractual clauses** | DPAs may be based on Commission or supervisory authority SCCs. |
| **Written form** | DPA must be in writing, including electronic form. |
| **Processor acting as controller** | If a processor determines purposes/means, it is treated as a controller for that processing. |

## Todo List

- [ ] Conduct a **processor inventory**: identify all third parties processing personal data on the organisation's behalf.
- [ ] Assess each processor's **guarantees of GDPR compliance** (questionnaire, audit rights, certifications, code of conduct adherence) before engagement or on renewal.
- [ ] Ensure a **compliant DPA** is in place with every processor, covering all Art. 28(3) mandatory elements.
- [ ] Decide on **specific vs. general written authorisation** for sub-processors; if general, implement a notification-and-objection mechanism.
- [ ] Obtain and maintain an up-to-date **list of authorised sub-processors** for each processor relationship.
- [ ] Verify DPA provisions require processors to **alert the controller** if an instruction would infringe GDPR.
- [ ] Ensure DPAs include an obligation for processors to **delete or return all data** at contract end.
- [ ] Include **audit and inspection rights** in all DPAs; exercise these rights periodically.
- [ ] Assess whether existing DPAs are based on **Commission or supervisory authority standard contractual clauses** (SCCs) and update if needed.
- [ ] Review processor DPAs when processors engage or change sub-processors to confirm sub-processor chains comply with Art. 28(4).
- [ ] Flag any processor that is determining its own purposes/means and reclassify as a **joint controller or independent controller** where appropriate.
