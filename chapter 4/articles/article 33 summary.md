# Art. 33 GDPR – Notification of a Personal Data Breach to the Supervisory Authority

## Summary

Article 33 imposes a **72-hour notification obligation** on controllers in the event of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons. Processors must notify their controller without undue delay on becoming aware of a breach.

### Notification Requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Deadline** | Without undue delay and, where feasible, within **72 hours** of becoming aware of the breach. |
| **Threshold** | Notification is required unless the breach is **unlikely to result in a risk** to rights and freedoms. |
| **Late notification** | If notification exceeds 72 hours, reasons for the delay must accompany the notification. |
| **Processor obligation** | Processor must notify the controller without undue delay upon becoming aware of a breach. |
| **Phased notification** | Where not all information is available, notification may be made in phases without further undue delay. |

### Mandatory Notification Content (Art. 33(3))

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **(a) Nature of breach** | Categories and approximate number of data subjects concerned; categories and approximate number of personal data records concerned. |
| **(b) Contact point** | Name and contact details of the DPO or other contact point. |
| **(c) Likely consequences** | Description of the likely consequences of the breach. |
| **(d) Measures taken/proposed** | Measures taken or proposed to address the breach and mitigate its adverse effects. |

### Documentation Obligation

All personal data breaches must be **documented** by the controller (facts, effects, remedial action taken) to enable supervisory authority verification of compliance, regardless of whether notification was required.
