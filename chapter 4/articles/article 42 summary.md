# Art. 42 GDPR – Certification

## Summary

Article 42 introduces a **voluntary GDPR certification framework** — data protection certification mechanisms, seals, and marks — through which controllers and processors can demonstrate compliance with specific processing operations. Certification is issued by accredited certification bodies (Art. 43) or, in some cases, the supervisory authority itself.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Voluntary** | Certification is voluntary and must be available via a transparent process. |
| **Who can obtain it** | Controllers and processors subject to GDPR, as well as non-EU entities using it as a transfer safeguard. |
| **Issued by** | Certification bodies (Art. 43) or the competent supervisory authority, based on criteria approved by the supervisory authority (Art. 58(3)) or the Board (Art. 63). |
| **European Data Protection Seal** | If the Board approves the criteria, a common EU-wide certification — the European Data Protection Seal — may be issued. |
| **Validity** | Maximum **3 years**; renewable if criteria continue to be met. |
| **Withdrawal** | Certification body or supervisory authority must withdraw it where criteria are no longer met. |
| **No reduced responsibility** | Certification does not reduce the controller's or processor's compliance obligations or liability. |
| **Third-country transfers** | Approved certifications may be used as a transfer safeguard under Art. 46(2)(f) by non-EU controllers/processors with binding commitments. |
| **Register** | The Board maintains a public register of all certification mechanisms, seals, and marks. |

### What Must Be Provided for Certification

The controller or processor must supply the certification body with **all information and access** to its processing activities necessary to conduct the certification procedure.
