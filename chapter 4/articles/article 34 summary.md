# Art. 34 GDPR – Communication of a Personal Data Breach to the Data Subject

## Summary

Article 34 requires controllers to **notify affected data subjects directly** when a personal data breach is likely to result in a **high risk** to their rights and freedoms. This obligation is distinct from — and additional to — the Art. 33 supervisory authority notification.

### Communication Obligation

| Requirement | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Threshold** | Applies where the breach is **likely to result in a HIGH risk** to the rights and freedoms of natural persons (higher bar than Art. 33). |
| **Timing** | Without undue delay. |
| **Form** | Clear and plain language. |
| **Content** | Must include the information from Art. 33(3)(b), (c) and (d): DPO/contact point, likely consequences, and measures taken/proposed. |

### Exceptions — No Communication Required If

| Exception | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **(a) Encryption/TOMs** | The controller implemented appropriate protection measures (e.g. encryption) that render data unintelligible to unauthorised persons. |
| **(b) Subsequent measures** | The controller has taken subsequent measures that ensure the high risk is no longer likely to materialise. |
| **(c) Disproportionate effort** | A public communication or similar measure must be used instead to inform data subjects equally effectively. |

### Supervisory Authority Role

The supervisory authority may **require the controller to communicate** the breach to data subjects, or may decide that one of the exceptions in paragraph 3 is met.
