# Chapter 2 GDPR – Principles (Art. 5–11)

## Overview

Chapter 2 establishes the **foundational principles** that underpin the entire GDPR framework. It defines how personal data must be processed (Art. 5), what makes processing lawful (Art. 6), and then provides specific rules for the two most common lawful bases—consent (Art. 7–8) and sensitive data (Art. 9–11). Controllers must satisfy both the general principles in Art. 5 and a specific lawful basis in Art. 6 for *every* processing activity.

---

## Article Summaries

### Art. 5 – Principles Relating to Processing of Personal Data
Six binding principles govern all processing: **lawfulness, fairness & transparency** · **purpose limitation** · **data minimisation** · **accuracy** · **storage limitation** · **integrity & confidentiality**. The controller bears the burden of proof for compliance (**accountability**).

### Art. 6 – Lawfulness of Processing
Processing is only lawful if at least one of six legal bases applies: **(a) consent**, **(b) contract**, **(c) legal obligation**, **(d) vital interests**, **(e) public task/official authority**, or **(f) legitimate interests** (not available to public authorities in their official capacity). Secondary/further processing must pass a compatibility test.

### Art. 7 – Conditions for Consent
When consent is the legal basis, it must be: **demonstrable** (controller bears the burden of proof), **distinguishable** from other declarations, **freely given** (no bundling with service conditions for unnecessary data), and **withdrawable** at any time as easily as it was given.

### Art. 8 – Child's Consent for Information Society Services
The default age of consent for online services is **16 years**. Member States may lower the threshold to a minimum of **13 years**. Below the threshold, **parental consent** is required and the controller must make reasonable verification efforts using available technology.

### Art. 9 – Special Categories of Personal Data
Processing of sensitive data (racial/ethnic origin, political opinions, religion, trade union membership, genetic/biometric data, health data, sexual orientation) is **prohibited by default**. Ten exceptions exist, including explicit consent, employment law, vital interests, non-profit activities, legal claims, public interest, and healthcare (under professional secrecy).

### Art. 10 – Criminal Convictions and Offences
Processing of criminal conviction/offence data may only be done **under official authority** or when specifically **authorised by Union/Member State law** with appropriate safeguards. Comprehensive criminal registers may only be held under official authority.

### Art. 11 – Processing Not Requiring Identification
Where a controller does not (or no longer) needs to identify data subjects, it has **no obligation to acquire additional identifying data**. In such cases, data subject rights under Art. 15–20 are suspended unless the individual provides sufficient information to enable identification.
