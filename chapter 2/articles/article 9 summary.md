# Art. 9 GDPR – Processing of Special Categories of Personal Data

## Summary

Article 9 imposes a **general prohibition** on processing *sensitive* personal data and then carves out **ten exceptions** under which such processing may be permitted.

### Categories covered by the prohibition (paragraph 1)
- Racial or ethnic origin
- Political opinions
- Religious or philosophical beliefs
- Trade union membership
- Genetic data
- Biometric data (used for unique identification)
- Health data
- Data concerning sex life or sexual orientation

### Exceptions (paragraph 2, points a–j)
| Point | Exception |
|---|---|
| (a) | Explicit consent by the data subject (unless national law prohibits lifting the ban) |
| (b) | Employment, social security & social protection law obligations/rights |
| (c) | Vital interests where the data subject is incapable of giving consent |
| (d) | Legitimate activities of a non-profit with political, philosophical, religious or trade-union aim (members/former members/regular contacts only; no external disclosure without consent) |
| (e) | Data manifestly made public by the data subject |
| (f) | Establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims / court judicial capacity |
| (g) | Substantial public interest under Union/Member State law (proportionate + safeguards) |
| (h) | Preventive/occupational medicine, medical diagnosis, health/social care (under professional secrecy – see § 3) |
| (i) | Public health interests (e.g., cross-border health threats, drug/device safety) under Union/Member State law |
| (j) | Archiving / scientific or historical research / statistics under Art. 89(1) + Union/Member State law |

**Paragraph 3:** Data under exception (h) must be processed by (or under the responsibility of) a professional bound by an obligation of secrecy.

**Paragraph 4:** Member States may add further conditions or limitations for genetic, biometric, or health data.
