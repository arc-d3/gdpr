# Art. 5 GDPR – Principles Relating to Processing of Personal Data

## Summary

Article 5 lays out the **six core principles** that must govern every processing activity involving personal data, plus an overarching accountability obligation on the controller.

| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Lawfulness, fairness & transparency** | Data must be processed on a valid legal basis, without deception, and in a way that is clear to the data subject. |
| b | **Purpose limitation** | Data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not processed incompatibly with those purposes. |
| c | **Data minimisation** | Only data that is adequate, relevant, and strictly necessary for the purpose may be processed. |
| d | **Accuracy** | Data must be accurate and kept up to date; inaccurate data must be erased or corrected without delay. |
| e | **Storage limitation** | Data must not be kept in identifiable form longer than necessary (exception: archiving/research/statistics under Art. 89). |
| f | **Integrity & confidentiality** | Appropriate technical and organisational security measures must protect data against unauthorised processing and accidental loss/damage. |

Paragraph 2 adds the **accountability principle**: the controller bears the burden of proof to demonstrate compliance with all of the above.
