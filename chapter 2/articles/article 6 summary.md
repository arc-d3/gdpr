# Art. 6 GDPR – Lawfulness of Processing

## Summary

Article 6 defines the **six legal bases** that make processing of personal data lawful. At least one must apply for any processing activity.

| Basis | Condition |
|---|---|
| **(a) Consent** | The data subject has given freely informed, specific, and unambiguous consent. |
| **(b) Contract** | Processing is necessary to perform a contract with the data subject or to take pre-contractual steps at their request. |
| **(c) Legal obligation** | Processing is necessary to comply with a legal obligation of the controller under Union or Member State law. |
| **(d) Vital interests** | Processing is necessary to protect the vital interests of the data subject or another natural person. |
| **(e) Public task** | Processing is necessary for a task in the public interest or exercise of official authority vested in the controller. |
| **(f) Legitimate interests** | Processing is necessary for the legitimate interests of the controller or a third party, unless overridden by the data subject's interests or fundamental rights (not available to public authorities acting in their official capacity). |

**Paragraph 2–3:** Member States may enact more specific rules for bases (c) and (e). The legal basis for (c)/(e) must be Union or Member State law that meets a public interest objective and is proportionate.

**Paragraph 4 – compatible further processing test:** Where further processing is not based on consent or a legal mandate, the controller must assess compatibility by considering: link between purposes, context of collection, nature of data (especially special-category data), consequences for data subjects, and existence of appropriate safeguards.
