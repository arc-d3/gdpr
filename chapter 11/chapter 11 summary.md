# Chapter 11 — Final Provisions

## Overview
Chapter 11 (Articles 94–99) closes the GDPR text with housekeeping and transitional provisions. It formally repeals the previous data protection directive, clarifies the relationship between GDPR and other EU legal instruments, establishes a periodic Commission review mechanism, and fixes the dates on which GDPR entered into force and became applicable. None of the articles in this chapter create ongoing compliance obligations for controllers or processors; they are primarily addressed to the EU legislature, the Commission, and Member States.

## Articles in this Chapter

| Article | Title |

## Key Themes
- **Repeal and continuity**: Directive 95/46/EC is repealed and all references to it — including references to the Article 29 Working Party — are automatically redirected to GDPR and the European Data Protection Board (EDPB).
- **Avoiding double regulation**: GDPR does not add obligations on top of Directive 2002/58/EC (ePrivacy Directive) for the same processing activities, ensuring sector-specific law governs electronic communications processing without duplication.
- **Grandfathering of existing agreements**: International data transfer agreements concluded before 24 May 2016 that were compliant with the then-applicable Union law remain in force until amended, replaced, or revoked.
- **Periodic review**: The Commission must periodically evaluate GDPR (starting 25 May 2020, every four years) and report publicly, with a focus on international transfers and the cooperation/consistency mechanism; it must propose amendments if needed.
- **Coherence across Union law**: The Commission is tasked with reviewing and aligning other Union legal acts on data protection to ensure uniform and consistent protection.
- **Dates**: GDPR entered into force on 24 May 2016 and became fully applicable on 25 May 2018.
