# Chapter 7 — Cooperation and Consistency

**Articles:** Articles 60–76

## Purpose
Chapter 7 establishes the mechanisms by which supervisory authorities across the EU cooperate and maintain consistent application of the GDPR. It is divided into three sections: Section 1 (Articles 60–62) governs direct cooperation between the lead supervisory authority and other concerned SAs; Section 2 (Articles 63–67) sets out the consistency mechanism through which the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) issues opinions, settles disputes, and handles urgent matters; Section 3 (Articles 68–76) establishes the EDPB itself — its composition, independence, tasks, internal procedures, secretariat, and confidentiality rules.

## Articles at a Glance
- **Article 60** — *Cooperation between the lead supervisory authority and the other supervisory authorities concerned*: The lead SA must cooperate with all concerned SAs, share draft decisions for comment, and escalate unresolved objections to the consistency mechanism.
- **Article 61** — *Mutual assistance*: SAs must provide each other with relevant information and assistance within one month; refusal is permitted only on narrow grounds; failure to respond triggers the urgency procedure.
- **Article 62** — *Joint operations of supervisory authorities*: SAs may conduct joint investigations and enforcement operations; liability for visiting staff rests with the host Member State, which is subsequently reimbursed by the seconding state.
- **Article 63** — *Consistency mechanism*: SAs must use the consistency mechanism (Articles 63–67) to ensure uniform GDPR application across the Union.
- **Article 64** — *Opinion of the Board*: The EDPB must issue opinions on a defined list of draft SA measures (e.g., DPIA lists, codes of conduct, BCRs, certification criteria) within eight weeks; SAs must take utmost account of the opinion.
- **Article 65** — *Dispute resolution by the Board*: Where SAs disagree on key matters, the EDPB adopts a binding decision by two-thirds majority within one to two months; the lead SA then issues its final decision on that basis.
- **Article 66** — *Urgency procedure*: In exceptional circumstances an SA may adopt provisional measures valid up to three months; urgent EDPB opinions or binding decisions can be obtained within two weeks.
- **Article 67** — *Exchange of information*: The Commission may adopt implementing acts to standardise the electronic format and procedures for information exchange between SAs and the Board.
- **Article 68** — *European Data Protection Board*: Formally establishes the EDPB as a Union body with legal personality, composed of the heads (or their representatives) of each Member State's SA and the European Data Protection Supervisor.
- **Article 69** — *Independence*: The EDPB acts independently and may not seek or receive instructions from any outside body (subject only to Commission requests under Article 70).
- **Article 70** — *Tasks of the Board*: Lists the broad catalogue of EDPB tasks, including issuing guidelines, recommendations, best practices, opinions, and binding decisions to ensure consistent GDPR application.
- **Article 71** — *Reports*: The EDPB must publish an annual report on data protection in the Union (and in third countries where relevant) and transmit it to the European Parliament, Council, and Commission.
- **Article 72** — *Procedure*: Decisions are taken by simple majority unless otherwise specified; rules of procedure are adopted by two-thirds majority.
- **Article 73** — *Chair*: The Board elects a Chair and two Deputy Chairs by simple majority for a renewable five-year term.
- **Article 74** — *Tasks of the Chair*: The Chair convenes and prepares Board meetings, notifies binding decisions, and ensures timely performance of the Board's tasks.
- **Article 75** — *Secretariat*: The EDPB secretariat is provided by the European Data Protection Supervisor under the Chair's instructions; it handles day-to-day operations, communications, translations, and preparation of Board outputs.
- **Article 76** — *Confidentiality*: Board discussions may be confidential where the Board so decides; access to documents is governed by Regulation (EC) No 1049/2001.

## Key Themes
- **One-stop-shop and lead authority cooperation**: Article 60 operationalises the one-stop-shop by requiring structured cooperation, consensus-seeking, and escalation of genuine disagreements to the EDPB.
- **Consistency mechanism**: Articles 63–67 form a layered system ensuring that no single national SA can unilaterally set binding precedent on matters affecting the whole Union.
- **EDPB as EU institution**: The EDPB is a genuine Union body with legal personality, independence from political instruction, and binding decision-making power (Article 65 decisions).
- **Speed and urgency**: The regime balances thoroughness (normal cooperation timelines) with the ability to act fast (two-week urgency opinions/decisions under Article 66).
- **Transparency and accountability**: Annual reports (Articles 71), public registers of decisions (Article 70(1)(y)), and public consultation obligations ensure democratic oversight of supranational data protection governance.

## Practical Implications
- Organisations with cross-border EU operations must understand that enforcement decisions require cooperation between multiple SAs; the lead SA's draft decision can be challenged and revised through the Article 60/65 process.
- The EDPB's binding decisions under Article 65 have direct legal effect on the concerned SAs and therefore on the organisations subject to those decisions.
- Guidelines, recommendations, and best practices issued under Article 70 carry significant de facto authority and are widely relied upon by compliance teams even though they are not formally binding law.
- Urgency measures under Article 66 can result in immediate provisional orders restricting processing before the full consistency procedure is completed.
- Companies developing codes of conduct, certification schemes, or binding corporate rules must engage with the EDPB opinion process under Article 64 before national SA approval.
