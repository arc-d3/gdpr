# Article 64 — Opinion of the Board

**Chapter:** 7 — Cooperation and Consistency

## Summary
Article 64 requires the EDPB to issue an opinion before a competent supervisory authority adopts certain significant measures. These include: lists of processing operations requiring a DPIA (Article 35(4)), compliance of codes of conduct (Article 40(7)), accreditation requirements (Articles 41(3), 43(3), 42(5)), standard data protection clauses (Article 46(2)(d) and Article 28(8)), contractual clauses for third-country transfers (Article 46(3)(a)), and binding corporate rules (Article 47). Any SA, the Board Chair, or the Commission may also request an opinion on any matter of general application or producing multi-state effects. The Board must issue its opinion within eight weeks (extendable by six weeks for complex matters) by simple majority. The requesting SA must give utmost account to the opinion and, if it does not intend to follow it, must refer the matter to the dispute resolution mechanism under Article 65.

## Key Obligations
- A competent SA **must submit draft decisions** in the six categories of Article 64(1) to the EDPB for an opinion.
- The SA **must not adopt** the draft decision during the Board's opinion period.
- Board issues opinion within **eight weeks** (extendable by six weeks); adopted by **simple majority**.
- Competent SA must **take utmost account** of the opinion.
- Within **two weeks** of receiving the opinion, the SA must inform the Chair whether it will maintain or amend its draft.
- Non-compliance with the opinion triggers **Article 65 dispute resolution**.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **EDPB opinion**: A formal position of the Board issued in response to a draft decision or general query; not directly binding, but refusal to follow it triggers binding dispute resolution under Article 65.
- **Standardised format**: The electronic format and procedures specified by the Commission under Article 67 for communication between SAs and the Board.

## Applies To
Supervisory authorities, the European Data Protection Board, the Commission.
