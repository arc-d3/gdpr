# Article 65 — Dispute Resolution by the Board

**Chapter:** 7 — Cooperation and Consistency

## Summary
Article 65 empowers the EDPB to adopt binding decisions where supervisory authorities cannot agree on cross-border matters. Binding decisions are required in three scenarios: (a) where a concerned SA has raised a relevant and reasoned objection that the lead SA rejected or did not follow; (b) where there are conflicting views on which SA is competent for the main establishment; and (c) where a competent SA has not sought a mandatory Article 64 opinion or has not followed an EDPB opinion. The EDPB must adopt its binding decision within one month (extendable by one month for complex matters) by a two-thirds majority; failing that, by simple majority within two further weeks, with the Chair casting the deciding vote if members are split. All concerned SAs are bound by the decision. The lead SA then adopts its final national decision within one month, attaching the EDPB binding decision.

## Key Obligations
- The EDPB **must issue a binding decision** in the three cases listed in Article 65(1).
- Decision adopted within **one month** (extendable by one month) by **two-thirds majority**; fallback: **simple majority** within two further weeks.
- Concerned SAs **must not adopt** their own decision while the matter is pending before the EDPB.
- Chair notifies the binding decision to all concerned SAs and the Commission.
- Lead SA must adopt its **final decision within one month** on the basis of the EDPB binding decision.
- The EDPB binding decision is **published on the Board's website**.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Binding decision**: An EDPB decision under Article 65 that is legally binding on all concerned supervisory authorities and must form the basis of the final SA decision.
- **Two-thirds majority**: The supermajority required to adopt a binding decision in the normal timeframe; reflects the seriousness of overriding a national SA's position.

## Applies To
Supervisory authorities, the European Data Protection Board, the Commission.
