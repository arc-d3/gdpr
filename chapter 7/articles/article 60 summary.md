# Article 60 — Cooperation Between the Lead Supervisory Authority and the Other Supervisory Authorities Concerned

**Chapter:** 7 — Cooperation and Consistency

## Summary
Article 60 is the operational core of the GDPR's one-stop-shop mechanism. It requires the lead supervisory authority (LSA) to cooperate with all other supervisory authorities concerned (CSAs) in an effort to reach consensus on cross-border cases. The LSA must share draft decisions with CSAs and allow them four weeks to raise relevant and reasoned objections. If an objection is not followed, the matter must be escalated to the EDPB via the consistency mechanism (Article 63). Once there are no outstanding objections, the decision is binding on all concerned SAs. The LSA then formally adopts and notifies the decision to the controller or processor, while SAs inform complainants. In exceptional circumstances, the urgency procedure under Article 66 may apply.

## Key Obligations
- LSA and CSAs must **exchange all relevant information** and seek consensus.
- LSA must **submit draft decisions** to CSAs and give them **four weeks** to object.
- Relevant and reasoned objections not followed must be referred to the **consistency mechanism** (Article 63/65).
- If following the objection, a revised draft is issued with a further **two-week** comment period.
- Where no objections remain, the LSA **adopts the final decision** and notifies the controller/processor and the Board.
- The SA where a complaint was lodged **informs the complainant** of the outcome.
- **Electronic communication** in a standardised format is required for all exchanges.

## Key Rights / Definitions
- **Lead supervisory authority (LSA)**: The SA at the controller's or processor's main establishment, competent to act as lead for cross-border processing (see Article 56).
- **Supervisory authorities concerned (CSAs)**: SAs of Member States where data subjects are substantially affected or where an establishment of the controller/processor is located.
- **Relevant and reasoned objection**: An objection that demonstrates significant risk of infringement or adverse effects on data subjects; must meet a substantive threshold.

## Applies To
Lead supervisory authorities, other supervisory authorities concerned, controllers and processors subject to cross-border enforcement.
